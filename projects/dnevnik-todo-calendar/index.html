Project Case Study

Dnevnik Todo Calendar

A weekly planner that treats digital planning like a paper ritual instead of a task feed.

Dnevnik Todo Calendar is a React, TypeScript, and Vite web app inspired by the structure of a Soviet and post-Soviet paper dnevnik. The interface is intentionally built like a two-page weekly spread: Monday through Wednesday on the left, Thursday through Saturday on the right. The goal is not to mimic paper for nostalgia alone, but to preserve the sense of order, rhythm, and restraint that makes notebook planning feel dependable.

The product combines that analog structure with modern utility. Users can drag tasks across days, expand rows within limits, open rich notes popups, search across tasks and notes, switch themes, and move between weekly and zoomed-out calendar views. Under the surface, it supports Supabase persistence with localStorage fallback, authentication, localization, and beta access control, so the planner feels thoughtful at both the UX and systems level.

Highlights

  • Planner structure: A square, notebook-inspired weekly spread with six default rows per day, current-day emphasis, and a layout designed to stay visually disciplined.
  • Task workflow: Rich notes per task, app-styled priority popups, drag and drop across days, a miscellaneous inbox for unsorted tasks, and multiple ways to close the notes editor without breaking flow.
  • Broader planning view: Month and year zoom-out modes with GitHub-style activity shading so planning history becomes visible instead of buried.
  • Product depth: English and Russian localization, multiple visual themes, separate auth flow, Google auth setup, invite-only beta access, and Vercel deployment for testing.

Stack: React, TypeScript, Vite, Supabase, localStorage fallback, Vercel

Why I Built It

I wanted to explore whether analog planning habits could be translated into software without losing their emotional logic. Most digital planners optimize for speed, density, and infinite flexibility. This project moves in a different direction: fewer choices at once, stronger weekly rhythm, and an interface that feels structured enough to support discipline instead of fragmenting attention.

That tension between nostalgia and modern product design is what makes the project interesting to me. It is intentionally quiet, but not simplistic. The planner tries to feel like a tool people return to because it supports a habit, not because it overwhelms them with features.

Future Direction

Today, Dnevnik Todo Calendar is a focused weekly planner. The longer-term opportunity is to expand it carefully into collaborative planning: shared boards across multiple users, worker or team assignments, deadlines, reminders, configurable email notifications for assignments and status changes, and richer workload or productivity statistics.

The direction is not to imitate heavyweight enterprise software, but to grow toward a calmer and more human-centered work-management layer. There is a credible path from a personal planner to a lightweight structured system for collaborative execution, while keeping the product visual, disciplined, and emotionally legible.
